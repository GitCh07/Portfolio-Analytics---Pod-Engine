How to read this. The deflated Sharpe asks whether the result beats the best Sharpe you would expect from 19 skill-less tries; every configuration ever run on this data is logged in state/trials.jsonl, so experimenting raises the hurdle automatically. The headline uses the stricter of two hurdles: one from the spread of the logged results (0.16, deflated Sharpe 100%) and one assuming every trial was an independent zero-skill strategy (0.37, 100%). Ideas you tried by hand before running the engine are not in the log, so even the strict figure is a ceiling. Above about 95% is strong, 80-95% is suggestive, below that the result is not distinguishable from luck. Overfitting probability is how often the best-looking setting in one half of history ended up below the median in the other half; under 20% is healthy, near 50% means choosing settings by backtest is no better than chance. When all the variants below score within noise of each other, a high value is expected and the lesson is to keep the defaults rather than switch to whichever variant happened to win.

Reading the tests. A p-value under 0.05 (marked ✗) rejects the model. Kupiec asks whether breaches happen as often as stated. 8. What this is, and what it is not

What is predictable. Volatility (roughly half of its variation), correlations (some), and how wide the range of outcomes is. The engine puts its modelling effort there. Direction is barely predictable, so expected returns are shrunk hard and no position depends on a point forecast.

Known weak points. (1) Free data only covers funds that exist today. (2) Positions are assumed to hold their target weight between rebalances. (3) Trading costs are flat assumptions per asset class; real costs rise in stress. (4) Value here means 'cheap versus five years ago', a price-only proxy. (5) Regime probabilities lag turning points. (6) Risk parity relies on stocks and bonds not falling together for long; 2022 is the cautionary case. (7) A backtest of a strategy designed after the fact always flatters it; that is what the deflated Sharpe and the trial log are for.

Not advice. This is a research tool. It does not know your tax situation, margin terms or other holdings.
