Reading the tests. A p-value under 0.05 (marked ✗) rejects the model. Kupiec asks whether breaches happen as often as stated. Christoffersen asks whether they bunch together, which means the model reacts too slowly. The Basel light counts 99% breaches in the last 250 days: 0-4 green, 5-9 yellow, 10 or more red. The self-corrected line adjusts its quantile after every breach, so it should pass the frequency test by design; the clustering test is the real check on it.

Expected Shortfall test (Acerbi-Székely Z2, filtered historical, 97.5%): Z2 = -0.153, p = 0.043 ✗. On the 147 days that broke the 97.5% VaR, the forecast ES averaged 2.26% and the actual loss averaged 2.28%. Z2 near 0 means the ES was right on average; clearly negative means tail losses were larger than forecast. The p-value is computed by simulating from the model's own daily scenarios.

Limits

The optimiser only rearranges what you hold (plus anything added with --add). It uses a 1-month risk forecast, so it is not built for intraday trading. Options positions are not supported, because their payoffs are not linear in the underlying price. Expected returns are estimates with wide error bars: treat the Black-Litterman column as 'what my views imply', not as a forecast. This is a research tool, not investment advice.
